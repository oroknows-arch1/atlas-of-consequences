# AOC-001 — Dialogue and Terminology Authenticity Lock v0.1

**Status:** Evidence-bounded language design lock.  
**Scope:** Spanish/work vocabulary and dialogue rules needed before prose drafting.  
**Date:** 2026-09-22.

## Core language rule

AOC-001 will be published primarily in English, but the characters live in Calama and would ordinarily speak Chilean Spanish.

The English prose should therefore read as **translation-transparent**: natural English that preserves the social and geographic reality without pretending the family literally speaks English to one another.

Do not decorate dialogue with random Chilean slang to prove where the characters live.

## Locked terms

Use Spanish terms only where they carry local meaning that an English replacement would flatten or where they are part of documented mining/education vocabulary.

### faena

**Approved.**

In Chilean mining usage, *faena* refers to the mining worksite/operation or work activity. It can appear sparingly in narration or dialogue when the context makes its meaning obvious.

First-use treatment can be:
**the faena — the mine worksite**

After that, *faena* may stand alone.

Do not translate it as "the mine" in every context because Mauricio may work for a contractor at an operation rather than belong to the mine owner.

### turno

**Approved.**

Use for a work shift/turn where natural. English narration can normally say **shift**; *turno* should appear only when the Spanish term itself matters.

### sistema 7x7 / turno 7x7

**Approved factual terminology.**

7x7 is documented in Chilean mining recruitment and employment material. In English prose prefer **7-on/7-off roster** on first explanation, while characters or supporting context can refer to **7x7**.

Do not assume every 7x7 roster has identical hours or day/night rotation.

### técnico / mantención / mantenimiento

**Controlled use.**

Chilean mining/employment sources use technical-maintenance terminology. The finished English edition should normally use **maintenance technician** and **maintenance**.

Do not mix regional Spanish variants casually. If a Spanish job title is eventually used, verify the exact title against the employer/context first.

### liceo

**Approved.**

For Tomás's education context, *liceo* can be retained when referring to a Chilean secondary school or a named institution. Do not make it equivalent to an Australian or US school category without explanation.

### enseñanza media / educación técnico-profesional (TP)

**Approved in factual/context sections.**

In STORY prose prefer natural language such as **secondary school** or **technical pathway** unless the institutional term matters.

## Household dialogue rule

Daniela, Mauricio, Tomás and Emilia should sound like four people in a family, not four representatives of Chile.

Their differences should come mainly from:
- age;
- relationship;
- mood;
- what each person knows;
- what they avoid saying;
- family habits.

Not from a quota of local expressions.

## Slang boundary

Do **not** insert unverified expressions such as *po*, *cachai*, *weón*, *al tiro*, *bacán* or other recognisably Chilean colloquialisms simply for flavour.

Some are genuinely used in Chile, but usage depends on age, relationship, register, region and context. A dictionary confirming that a word exists is not enough to establish that Daniela or Mauricio would use it in a particular scene.

If later dialogue needs colloquial Spanish:
1. identify the exact scene/register;
2. check contemporary Chilean usage from credible linguistic/local sources;
3. use the minimum necessary;
4. have a Chilean/local authenticity reader review it before publication where possible.

## Names and forms of address

Keep the established provisional names:
- Daniela Rojas
- Mauricio Soto
- Tomás
- Emilia

Do not invent nicknames yet.

Spanish accents must be preserved where applicable, including **Tomás**.

Do not infer full legal naming structures, maternal surnames, diminutives or family nicknames unless the story needs them.

## Translation rule for publication

The master STORY may be written in English for Atlas's audience while representing conversations that occur in Spanish.

Atlas should not use constructions such as:

> "Vamos," Mauricio said. "Let's go."

unless bilingualism itself matters to the scene.

Choose one readable layer. For the English edition, translate ordinary dialogue into natural English and retain only selected Spanish terms that carry useful local meaning.

A later Spanish edition should be translated/localised as its own editorial product rather than generated by sprinkling Spanish into the English master.

## Work-language safeguard

Mauricio's technical dialogue should be no more specialised than the scene requires.

Do not invent:
- equipment faults;
- safety procedures;
- maintenance instructions;
- mine-site radio language;
- contractor jargon;
- technical acronyms.

If a scene depends on a technical detail, research that detail separately before prose is locked.

This prevents plausible-sounding invented mining practice from quietly becoming fact.

## Child-language safeguard

Emilia's speech must remain age appropriate. She should not use roster terminology to explain the family theme.

Tomás can understand the broad meaning of Mauricio's roster and career but should not automatically speak like a mining technician.

## Evidence boundary

### Supported reality

Terms such as *faena*, *turno* and 7x7 are established in Chilean mining/work contexts. Chilean education sources use *liceo*, *enseñanza media* and Technical-Professional terminology.

### Fiction

Every line of dialogue, family expression, nickname, joke and interpersonal usage is invented.

### Prohibited inference

A term being common in Chile does not prove a particular fictional Calama resident would use it. Atlas must not present generic Chilean slang as specifically Calameño without evidence.

## Editorial style

**Locality should be felt through the world before it is heard through slang.**

Calama becomes convincing through geography, work rhythms, institutions, climate, routines, relationships and researched material conditions. Dialogue then only needs to sound human.
