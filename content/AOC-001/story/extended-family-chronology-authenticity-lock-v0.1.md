# AOC-001 — Extended Family / Chuquicamata Chronology Lock v0.1

**Status:** Evidence-supported fictional design lock.  
**Scope:** Whether and how AOC-001 may use an older relative with lived memory of the former Chuquicamata camp.  
**Date:** 2026-09-22.

## Decision

**Retain one older relative, but keep the character secondary in AOC-001.**

Daniela's mother — provisionally **Elena Rojas, 64** — lived in the former Chuquicamata camp as an adult and moved to Calama during the organised relocation period.

At 64 in 2026, Elena would have been about 39–45 during the principal 2001–2007 relocation years. That makes adult memory of the move chronologically plausible without requiring Atlas to invent childhood memories from a period the character could not remember.

Her name and age remain fictional editorial choices; the historical relocation does not.

## Historical anchor

Codelco records establish that:
- the Nueva Calama project began at the end of 2002 to relocate more than 10,000 people from the Chuquicamata camp to Calama;
- the wider relocation was already underway from about 2001;
- housing, schools and other infrastructure were built/transferred during the process;
- the residential camp closed in 2007, with the last families leaving that year;
- Codelco reported the relocation as complete by early 2008.

The process involved thousands of families and was not merely a change of address. Contemporary Codelco material describes social, cultural, environmental and urban-integration dimensions.

## Character role

Elena must **not** become a history narrator.

She exists first as Daniela's mother and the children's grandmother.

Her ordinary story functions can include:
- helping with a practical family task;
- having her own plans and availability rather than being automatic childcare;
- teasing or disagreeing with Daniela;
- knowing Mauricio independently of the roster conflict;
- having a relationship with Tomás and Emilia;
- remembering the old camp only when something naturally prompts it.

If Chuquicamata history enters her dialogue, keep it personal and bounded:
- a place she remembers;
- a routine that changed;
- a comparison between then and now;
- an object, photograph or family habit.

Do not use Elena to deliver paragraphs of exposition about mining history.

## Biography boundary

For v0.1, lock only:
- Elena is Daniela's mother;
- she is approximately 64 in 2026;
- she lived in the former Chuquicamata camp as an adult;
- she moved to Calama during the 2001–2007 relocation era;
- she still lives in or near Calama closely enough to be part of the family's ordinary life.

Do **not** yet invent:
- whether Elena or her partner worked for Codelco;
- her exact house in the camp;
- which relocation housing sector she moved to;
- compensation/property arrangements;
- a precise relocation year;
- school or hospital experiences;
- political attitudes toward the relocation;
- whether Daniela herself lived in the camp.

That last point is especially important. Daniela would have been roughly 13–19 across 2001–2007, so it is chronologically possible that she experienced part of the relocation too. AOC-001 should not accidentally establish that biography until we deliberately decide whether it helps the story.

## Why Elena stays

The character gives the household generational depth without requiring a second crisis.

She can quietly establish that mining-related change did not begin with AI, Mauricio or the present story. Calama families have already lived through major changes in how mining, work and city life fit together.

But AOC-001 does **not** need to argue that the historical relocation and Mauricio's fictional opportunity are equivalent. They are different events at radically different scales.

Elena's presence mainly prevents the Rojas/Soto household from behaving like an isolated four-person unit.

## Evidence boundary

### Supported reality

The Chuquicamata residential relocation to Calama occurred over the 2000s, involving more than 10,000 people / thousands of families, with the camp's residential life ending in 2007.

### Fiction

Elena Rojas, her age, family relationships, residence, memories, opinions and participation in the relocation are invented.

### Prohibited inference

Do not take Codelco's institutional account as proof of how every relocated resident experienced the process. If Elena expresses nostalgia, relief, resentment, affection or ambivalence, those are her fictional feelings unless a factual section separately attributes documented views to real sources.

## Story-use limit

AOC-001 gets **at most one brief Chuquicamata-memory beat** from Elena unless the finished narrative proves it genuinely needs more.

The first story remains about Daniela and Mauricio's present household decision.

## Sources

- Codelco, “Con nostalgia de Chuqui” (29 Aug 2007) — roughly 3,000 families had left the camp since 2001.
- Codelco, “Chuquicamata: 92 años de historia” — more than 10,000 people moved to Calama and the residential camp closed.
