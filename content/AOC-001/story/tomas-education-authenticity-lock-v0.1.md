# AOC-001 — Tomás Education/Future Authenticity Lock v0.1

**Status:** Evidence-supported fictional design lock.  
**Scope:** Tomás's age-appropriate education and future-choice architecture.  
**Date:** 2026-09-22.

## Locked baseline

Tomás Soto, 16, is still in **Enseñanza Media** in Calama.

He is old enough for the question of what comes after school to be becoming concrete, but Atlas must not write him as though he is already selecting a permanent adult career.

His story position is **exploration, not commitment**.

## Local education reality

Calama has both Humanistic-Scientific and Technical-Professional secondary education.

Current Ministry of Education records confirm local establishments including:
- **Liceo Minero América**, offering Humanistic-Scientific and industrial Technical-Professional secondary education;
- **Liceo Técnico Profesional Jorge Alessandri Rodríguez**, offering Humanistic-Scientific, commercial TP and industrial TP secondary education.

Chile's Technical-Professional secondary framework includes specialities relevant to the regional economy, including Electricity, Electronics, mining specialities, Administration and technology/communications fields.

Technical-Professional higher education is also a real post-school pathway. For 2027 admission, the minimum general entry requirement is a secondary-school licence (Licencia de Enseñanza Media), and students from related secondary TP specialities can in some cases receive subject recognition through the national articulation framework.

These facts establish genuine choices. They do not determine what Tomás studies.

## Locked story choice

Tomás is **not yet locked into a mining speciality**.

His immediate future question is whether to move toward a technical pathway that could connect naturally to Calama's mining economy, or preserve a broader route that could lead elsewhere.

### Mining-linked route

The clearest fictional route is an interest in **electricity/electronics or industrial technical work**.

This deliberately avoids making Tomás a copy of Mauricio. Mauricio's maintenance career gives Tomás a visible example of where technical competence can lead, but Tomás can be interested in electrical/electronic systems rather than his father's exact mechanical-maintenance work.

A later route could plausibly continue through secondary TP study and/or post-secondary technical education before employment.

### Genuine alternative

Tomás must also have a credible non-mining-specific option.

For v0.1, keep that alternative broad: **Humanistic-Scientific secondary study followed by higher education**, with his eventual field unresolved.

This matters narratively. The choice is not:
- mining job versus unemployment;
- Calama versus escape;
- father's life versus rejecting father.

It is:
**specialise earlier toward a technical economy he can already see, or keep more future directions open.**

## Mauricio and Daniela

Mauricio can see technical education as concrete, employable and understandable. His own step-up opportunity strengthens that evidence inside the household.

He should not pressure Tomás to become a miner.

Daniela can value the same technical pathway while wanting Tomás's choice to remain genuinely his. Her concern is not anti-mining; it is whether the family's economic environment quietly makes one future feel inevitable.

Neither parent gets the correct answer.

## Tomás's own position

Tomás should have reasons beyond parental expectations.

Permitted:
- he genuinely likes practical/technical problem-solving;
- he notices the earning/career potential around him;
- he is curious about systems, electronics, machines or technology;
- he also sees Mauricio's absences and understands that a career has a life pattern attached to it;
- friends may be considering different routes.

Do not yet invent his school, grades, university ambition, apprenticeship, scholarship, favourite subject or exact speciality unless later evidence/story need justifies it.

## Story function

Mauricio's opportunity now affects Tomás without requiring a speech about copper.

The offer demonstrates that technical mining-linked competence can lead somewhere tangible.

That lets a small domestic moment carry the theme: a course, school choice, information page, conversation about next year, or question about what Mauricio actually does can make Tomás's future newly concrete.

The reader should feel:

**The same opportunity that asks more of Mauricio's family time can make his working life look more attractive as a model for his son.**

That contradiction is the point.

## Evidence boundary

### Supported reality

- Calama has current Humanistic-Scientific and Technical-Professional secondary education.
- Local TP/industrial education exists.
- Chile's recognised TP specialities include electrical/electronic, mining, administrative and technology-related routes.
- Chile has post-secondary Technical-Professional pathways, with secondary-school completion as the general entry requirement and articulation possibilities for some secondary TP graduates.

### Fiction

Tomás, his school, interests, academic performance, conversations, eventual study route and career choice are invented.

### Prohibited inference

Do not claim Calama teenagers generally prefer mining, reject mining, follow their parents into mining, or choose technical education because of mining wages without appropriate evidence.

Do not present Tomás as a demographic trend.

## Details intentionally not locked

- his named school;
- current year level;
- exact TP speciality;
- grades;
- university/CFT/IP destination;
- friends' educational plans;
- whether he ultimately stays in Calama;
- final occupation.

Those are future story/world decisions, not requirements for AOC-001.
