# AOC-001 — Emilia Childhood Routine Authenticity Lock v0.1

**Status:** Evidence-bounded fictional design lock.  
**Scope:** Emilia's age-seven school/family routine and the behavioural expression of Mauricio's absence.  
**Date:** 2026-09-22.

## Locked baseline

Emilia Soto, 7, is a **primary-school-aged child in Calama**.

Atlas does not need to name her school, class, teacher or neighbourhood in v0.1. Her world should be written at child scale: getting ready for school, carrying school things, snacks/meals, homework or something to show an adult, friends, play, sibling friction, bedtime and small plans that matter enormously to her.

Her function is not to explain the family's roster. She experiences it.

## School-age reality

At seven, Emilia plausibly sits in the early years of Chilean **Educación Básica**. Chile's school system formally separates Educación Básica from Educación Media, and Calama has municipal/public and other primary-school provision.

For AOC-001, that institutional reality is enough. The story does not require an invented school timetable or a claim that one Calama school's routine represents all children.

## Locked household routine

When Mauricio is away, Daniela is the physically present parent through the ordinary weekday rhythm.

Because Daniela also works, the story must not imply she spends the entire day waiting around the home. Household and school logistics have to fit around her employment and whatever support arrangement is eventually selected.

Tomás is an older sibling, not a substitute parent. He may help in small ordinary ways without being made responsible for raising Emilia.

Mauricio remains involved while away through plausible remote contact, but Atlas should not force a perfectly scheduled daily video-call ritual unless a later scene needs and supports it.

## Locked behavioural consequence

The primary small behaviour is now:

**When Mauricio has recently returned home, Emilia automatically takes a minor everyday need to Daniela first even though Mauricio is available.**

The need should be trivial enough that no explanation is required—for example help finding something, checking something for school, opening/fixing a small item, or asking permission.

Mauricio notices.

Emilia does not.

Daniela may notice but does not need to explain it.

This is not rejection of Mauricio. It is habit produced inside the fictional household's away rhythm.

Later, include a separate warm Mauricio–Emilia interaction so the story makes clear that physical absence has not been converted into emotional abandonment.

## Why this is the right scale

Avoid giving Emilia thematic dialogue such as asking why her father always chooses work.

A seven-year-old's ordinary behaviour can carry the consequence more credibly than an adult-sounding speech.

The moment should last seconds. Atlas can let the reader understand it without interpreting it on the family's behalf.

## Permitted ordinary material

Subject to scene-level checking, Emilia can plausibly:
- prepare for or return from school;
- carry schoolwork or something she wants an adult to see;
- ask an adult for permission/help;
- play or argue with Tomás;
- communicate with Mauricio while he is away;
- be excited by his return;
- resume Daniela-first habits without consciously choosing between parents;
- have friends/interests unrelated to mining.

## Do not invent for atmosphere

Do not introduce:
- a named school or precise timetable without checking it;
- specific school fees;
- transport arrangements;
- after-school care;
- a grandparent/cousin childcare system;
- local slang in Emilia's dialogue;
- a claim that Calama mining families commonly show this behaviour;
- emotional or developmental diagnoses.

Those can be added only if the story later needs them and evidence/context supports them.

## Evidence boundary

### Supported reality

Emilia's age is compatible with early Educación Básica in Chile, and primary education exists locally in Calama.

### Fiction

Emilia, her school life, routines, family habits, behaviour toward Daniela and Mauricio, friendships, dialogue and all incidents are invented.

### Interpretive boundary

The Daniela-first moment is a fictional illustration of one plausible family adaptation to repeated physical absence. It is **not evidence** that children of roster workers generally behave this way.

## Character safeguard

Emilia must appear in at least one moment that has nothing to do with Mauricio's absence or the Atlas thesis.

If every scene involving her exists to demonstrate the consequence, she has become a symbol rather than a child.
