# AOC-001 — Daniela Work Authenticity Lock v0.1

**Status:** Evidence-supported fictional design lock.  
**Scope:** Daniela's occupation and work rhythm only.  
**Date:** 2026-09-22.

## Locked character baseline

Daniela Rojas, 38, works in a **Calama-based administrative coordination/support role** for a local private-sector organisation outside the mine site.

Her work is ordinary, skilled and necessary: records and documents, calls/messages, scheduling, coordination, resolving routine problems, and dealing with colleagues or customers/users. She is not written as Mauricio's economic dependent or as a generic "wife at home."

The employer remains fictional and unnamed. Atlas should not imply a real Calama organisation employs Daniela.

## Why this occupation fits

Contemporary Calama employment evidence includes full-time administrative work requiring document/platform use, communication, collaboration, problem solving and handling workplace pressure. This supports an administrative/support occupation as plausible in the city.

Chile's ordinary-work framework also gives the story a useful contemporary boundary: from 26 April 2026 the ordinary maximum work week is 42 hours, generally distributed over five or six days, with a 10-hour daily maximum. Atlas should not casually write an outdated 45-hour ordinary week.

## Locked fictional work rhythm

Daniela works a **regular weekday daytime schedule**, broadly Monday to Friday, within the contemporary 42-hour ordinary-work framework.

Exact start/finish times are intentionally not locked because they depend on the fictional employer and are unnecessary unless a scene requires them.

Her work is local rather than a mining camp roster. This creates a deliberately different rhythm from Mauricio:

- Daniela's employment continues every week whether Mauricio is home or away;
- school-day and household logistics occur around her own work commitments;
- when Mauricio is away she cannot simply become a full-time household manager;
- when he returns, Daniela's weekday obligations do not disappear because he is on rest days.

This difference is central to the household architecture.

## What Mauricio's opportunity now means for Daniela

Mauricio's proposed move from an 8x6-style rhythm to rotating 7x7 day/night work does not merely create "more chores" for Daniela.

It changes **which parts of ordinary life the couple can share**.

A seven-day away block can contain an entire school week. A home block can overlap Daniela's working week. Following night rotation, some nominally "home" time can also be recovery time.

That gives Atlas several credible, small-scale pressures without inventing a crisis:

- an appointment or school issue lands while Daniela is at work and Mauricio is away;
- Mauricio is home during a weekday but Daniela cannot simply take the day off;
- a household decision is made before Mauricio can participate physically;
- Daniela has built a working routine during the away block and must readjust when he returns;
- the couple can have more total time together on paper than their usable shared time suggests.

These are fictional possibilities, not claims about typical Calama households.

## Daniela's independent life

The occupation must do more than service the roster plot.

Daniela should have:
- colleagues and workplace relationships;
- tasks she is good at;
- occasional frustrations and successes unrelated to mining;
- her own employment choices and ambitions;
- practical reasons she cannot automatically reorganise her work around Mauricio.

Her private ambition remains consistent with the character architecture: she wants the household to reach a position where major choices are made because the family wants them, rather than because one pay packet or roster dictates them.

Do not make her job deliberately low-status to exaggerate Mauricio's mining income. Do not make her career unrealistically flexible merely to solve scene logistics.

## Evidence boundary

### Supported reality

- Full-time administrative employment exists in contemporary Calama.
- Such roles can involve documentation/platforms, communication, collaboration and problem solving.
- From 26 April 2026, Chile's ordinary maximum work week is 42 hours, ordinarily distributed over five or six days, subject to applicable labour arrangements.

### Fiction

Daniela Rojas, her employer, her exact duties, Monday-Friday pattern, workplace relationships, salary, household responsibilities and every incident in the story are invented.

### Prohibited inference

Do not present Daniela as representative of Calama women, mining partners, working mothers or Chilean households generally.
