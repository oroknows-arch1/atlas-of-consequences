One conceptual issue is deliberately preserved rather than "solved": 7x7 is not automatically more absence than 8x6. The story must therefore portray the cost through **rotation, recovery, timing and usable shared time**, not falsely claim that seven days away is numerically longer than eight.

That is a feature of the story, not a blocker.

## Facts the draft must not invent

During first-draft prose, stop and research rather than guess if a scene requires:
- exact mine-site shift hours;
- exact 7x7 day/night rotation sequence;
- technical maintenance procedure or equipment fault;
- named contractor/employer;
- named neighbourhood/street;
- exact commute duration or worker pickup point;
- named school or school timetable;
- childcare arrangement;
- precise salaries/expenses/property status;
- Chilean slang or specialist jargon not already approved;
- a serious local social condition such as crime, abuse, addiction, discrimination, pollution, housing pressure or water hardship.

If the scene works without the detail, omit it.

## Drafting brief

The first story draft should:
- enter fiction through Daniela;
- show the household functioning while Mauricio is away;
- make Mauricio's return warm but slightly disruptive;
- use Emilia's Daniela-first moment quietly;
- introduce the opportunity as genuinely good news;
- make the economic benefit concrete through future optionality rather than luxury;

- avoid a catastrophic secondary plot;
- let the family move toward accepting the opportunity;
- end with ordinary life resuming and the central question unresolved.

Keep the household section lean enough that every scene earns its place.
