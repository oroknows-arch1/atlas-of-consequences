# AOC-001 — Antofagasta Local Context Pack v0.1

**Research status:** Initial context pack complete enough to select the v0.1 human viewpoint. Further local research is required before drafting STORY.

## Viewpoint recommendation

**Anchor the first story in Calama, with a mining-worker/family connection to Chuquicamata and the wider Antofagasta Region.**

Do not make the protagonist an Indigenous community member in v0.1 unless substantially deeper community-specific research and appropriate local sourcing are completed.

## Why Calama

Calama is the strongest human bridge between global copper demand and everyday life.

- Censo 2024 counted 166,334 people in Calama, 26.2% of the Antofagasta Region's population.
- Chuquicamata is about 15 km north of Calama. Codelco reports 3,992 direct employees at the division at the end of 2025 and 266 thousand metric tonnes of fine copper production in 2025.
- Mining has already physically reshaped family life here. More than 10,000 residents of the former Chuquicamata company town were relocated to Calama as the mine expanded and environmental/operational conditions changed.
- Long-distance labour commuting is a documented feature of the regional mining economy. UCN research identifies high housing/service costs, service quality and environmental concerns as factors influencing workers' decisions about whether their families live in Antofagasta or Calama.
- This gives Atlas a grounded recurring question: when global demand values what a place produces, how much of that value becomes a durable life in the place itself?

## Regional frame

Censo 2024 counted 635,416 people in the Antofagasta Region:
- Antofagasta commune: 401,096 (63.1%)
- Calama: 166,334 (26.2%)
- Tocopilla: 25,400
- Mejillones: 14,084
- Taltal: 12,097
- San Pedro de Atacama: 9,843
- María Elena: 4,834
- Sierra Gorda: 1,472
- Ollagüe: 256

Antofagasta has one of Chile's younger regional age structures. It also recorded negative recent internal migration in Censo 2024: more people moved from the region to another Chilean region than moved into it from elsewhere in Chile.

## Work and money

Mining is structurally central to the region, but "mining region" must not be treated as meaning everyone is a miner.

INE's 2024 income survey reported:
- mean monthly net labour income: CLP 1,056,125
- median monthly net labour income: CLP 757,312
- male mean: CLP 1,229,130
- female mean: CLP 807,421

The gap between high mining-linked incomes and the wider lived economy is potentially relevant to character construction, but must be researched locally rather than assumed.

UCN reports that long-distance commuting is reinforced by high housing and service costs, limited perceived quality of health/education/recreation services, and environmental concerns. Earlier UCN work estimated roughly 16% of the region's total workforce did not reside in the region. Treat the older 16% figure as historical context, not a current statistic.

## Housing

Censo 2024 reports Antofagasta among the Chilean regions with relatively high overcrowding:
- 7.9% of dwellings overcrowded
- 1.2% critically overcrowded

The share of apartments in the region increased by 10 percentage points between 2002 and 2024.

These regional facts do not establish that mining or AI caused housing pressure. They establish housing as a real local condition requiring deeper Calama-level research before use in STORY.

## Water and desert infrastructure

Water is part of everyday infrastructure in one of the world's driest environments.

The region contains established and proposed desalination infrastructure for urban and industrial uses. Mining projects increasingly include desalinated-water systems or hydrological transitions.

Do not collapse "desert", "mining" and "water conflict" into a single dramatic trope. Identify the exact water source, user, infrastructure and dispute for any factual claim.

## Place and mining history

Chuquicamata's industrial history extends back to the early twentieth century, while copper use and extraction in the area long predates industrial mining.

The former Chuquicamata company town is especially relevant to Atlas. Its closure and relocation of more than 10,000 residents to Calama demonstrates, historically and concretely, how a resource industry can reshape where families live, what infrastructure a city needs, and how work and community become intertwined.

Chuquicamata is now protected in part as a National Monument/heritage area.

## Indigenous and cultural context

El Loa province contains numerous legally constituted Indigenous communities, predominantly Atacameño/Lickanantay, as well as Aymara and Quechua communities. Their relationships to territory, water, development and mining cannot be treated as generic "Indigenous opposition" or used as atmosphere.

For v0.1:
- do not invent an Indigenous viewpoint merely to add cultural weight;
- use Indigenous histories and contemporary issues only where supported by community-specific or authoritative evidence;
- preserve local names and terminology accurately;
- distinguish Calama urban life from nearby rural/ancestral communities;
- deepen research before placing fictional events inside a specific community.

## Human themes supported by evidence

Potential themes, not predetermined plot:
- work that pays well but pulls people away from home;
- whether a resource city receives enough of the prosperity generated around it;
- family decisions about where to live;
- pride in mining alongside concern about quality of life;
- inherited mining identity versus changing aspirations;
- a city repeatedly adapting to the physical requirements of extraction;
- global demand arriving locally without local people necessarily seeing or naming its distant source.

## What we should NOT write yet

Do not state as fact that:
- AI is currently driving a particular Antofagasta mine expansion;
- AI has increased Calama rents;
- AI has caused migration into or out of Calama;
- AI has caused local water shortages;
- a particular community supports or opposes a project;
- mining workers universally want to leave Calama;
- Calama is simply poor, wealthy, dangerous, polluted or culturally homogeneous.

Each requires specific evidence.

## Viewpoint decision for the next stage

The best v0.1 story lens is **a Calama household connected to copper work**, rather than Antofagasta city as the primary setting.

The household structure and protagonist remain intentionally undecided. Before character creation, conduct one more narrow research pass into contemporary Calama daily life, local voices, neighbourhood geography, shift patterns, family routines, language, aspirations and current debates.

That pass should answer: **Who could plausibly live this consequence without being constructed to serve the thesis?**

## Source caution

Codelco is a primary source for its operations and its own historical programs, but it is also the mining company involved. Its descriptions of motives, benefits and community outcomes should not be treated as independent evaluation. Local reporting, academic work, public statistics, environmental records and community sources should be used to test and broaden the account.
