# AOC001-STORY-PROMO — Production Lock

**Edition:** AOC-001 — *AI's Physical Hunger*  
**Asset:** `AOC001-STORY-PROMO`  
**Role:** cinematic fictional story teaser  
**Status:** PRODUCTION DIRECTION LOCKED / NOT YET PRODUCED  
**Parent authority:** `ATLAS-BUILD-LOCK.md`

This file is the detailed production contract for `AOC001-STORY-PROMO`. It supplements the story-promo section of `ATLAS-BUILD-LOCK.md`. If a production decision conflicts with the parent build lock or the approved story, stop rather than reinterpret the story.

## 1. Core purpose — LOCKED

`AOC001-STORY-PROMO` is a short cinematic teaser for the **fictional human story** inside AOC-001.

Its job is not to explain the EMRADAR consequence chain again. The factual report already does that.

Its job is to make the audience feel the human question inside the story:

> **What does providing for your family mean when the opportunity that helps you provide also asks for more of your absence?**

The promo should create interest in Daniela, Mauricio and their family without summarising the whole story or resolving its tension.

## 2. AI-generation permission — EXPLICITLY LOCKED

The story promo **may be fully AI-generated**.

AI-generated fictional people, environments, performances, camera movement, lighting, weather, sound design, transitions and cinematic imagery are permitted because this asset is explicitly fiction.

Unlike the factual report, the promo does **not** require documentary source provenance for every visual shot.

The production is therefore free to use the strongest available generative tools and cinematic techniques, provided the output remains true to the approved story and is clearly labelled as fiction.

### Production freedom includes
- generating the fictional family and supporting fictional people;
- generating homes, streets, interiors, mine-related worker environments and Calama atmosphere consistent with the approved story world;
- choosing camera angles, lenses, framing and movement;
- choosing naturalistic lighting, time of day and weather consistent with the scene;
- creating cinematic transitions, match cuts and visual motifs;
- condensing story moments into trailer language;
- using reaction shots, details, hands, objects, rooms, transport, uniforms and environmental inserts that support the existing story;
- creating music, ambience, room tone and sound design later as part of the Atlas audio/brand language;
- using little or no dialogue if imagery and sound carry the emotion more effectively.

These freedoms are **production freedoms, not story freedoms**.

## 3. Story truth — HARD BOUNDARY

The approved story is the source of truth.

The promo may **compress, select and juxtapose** moments from the story, but it must not create a new plot.

It must not invent for dramatic effect:
- a promotion, job offer or mine expansion not present in the approved story;
- a direct causal link between Anthropic/AI demand and Mauricio's work circumstances;
- an accident, injury, death, crime, betrayal, abuse or family crisis that the story does not contain;
- new romantic or family relationships;
- a different household structure;
- a different occupation for Daniela or Mauricio;
- a different chronology that changes what the story means;
- a different city, mine or cultural setting;
- dialogue that materially changes a character's motives or the story's outcome.

**The promo may dramatise existing truth. It may not manufacture higher stakes.**

## 4. Character continuity — LOCKED

The core fictional family is:
- **Daniela**
- **Mauricio**
- **Tomás**
- **Emilia**
- **Elena**

Before moving-shot generation, production must establish a reusable visual character bible / reference set so these characters remain recognisable across shots.

Character continuity includes:
- approximate age and life stage;
- facial identity and body type;
- hair and defining visual traits;
- relationship to the other characters;
- work role where relevant;
- plausible wardrobe and workwear;
- home and economic context;
- emotional temperament established by the story.

Generated shots fail if characters visibly change identity, age, ethnicity, household role or core appearance without a narrative reason.

The characters are **fictional people**. Do not intentionally reproduce the likeness of a real identifiable person.

## 5. Place truth — LOCKED

The story is set in **Calama, Antofagasta Region, Chile**, within the social and economic gravity of the northern Chilean copper economy.

AI generation has freedom to create cinematic Calama scenes, but the city must remain believable as the place established by the story research.

The promo should be capable of showing both:
- the industrial/mining world that shapes household rhythms; and
- ordinary lived Calama — home, streets, family routines, school/community life and domestic spaces.

Do not reduce Calama to empty desert, mine machinery or a generic Latin-American visual stereotype.

The existing story authenticity locks remain the reference for geography, work, household economics, dialogue/terminology and childhood context.

## 6. Visual style — LOCKED

Use the **second user-supplied reference clip** as the production-language benchmark.

The desired style is:
- cinematic;
- intimate;
- emotionally led;
- naturalistic rather than glossy advertising;
- contemporary and premium;
- human faces, gestures and environment over explanatory graphics;
- strong use of place, light, texture, silence and small details;
- coherent enough to feel like one film world rather than a string of unrelated AI clips.

### Desired cinematic language
Possible visual language includes:
- early light through a family home;
- work clothes / boots / lunch preparation / keys / transport;
- a parent leaving while the household is still waking or settling;
- a child watching, waiting or continuing an ordinary routine;
- Daniela handling the practical rhythm of the household;
- Mauricio in work-related transition moments rather than heroic mine spectacle;
- Calama streets and domestic details;
- distance expressed through empty chairs, doorways, phone screens, windows, shift timing or missed moments;
- warmth inside the family contrasted with harder industrial scale outside it.

These are **production possibilities**, not mandatory shots and not permission to invent events.

## 7. Text and dialogue — LIGHT TOUCH

The promo should not explain the story with paragraphs or title cards.

Prefer:
- very short dialogue fragments already consistent with the story;
- one-line emotional text where useful;
- environmental sound;
- pauses and facial performance;
- visual implication.

The audience should want to read/watch the story because the promo creates a question, not because the promo has already narrated the answer.

## 8. Fiction labelling — NON-NEGOTIABLE

The promo must be visibly identified as **`STORY / FICTION`** at its opening or immediately on entry in a way that a fast-scrolling viewer cannot reasonably miss.

The label may be elegant and restrained, but it cannot be hidden.

The promo must never be styled to imply:
- witness footage;
- documentary interviews;
- CCTV;
- real family archival footage;
- actual miners or real Calama residents being depicted.

AI-generated realism is allowed. **Misrepresentation as documentary evidence is not.**

## 9. Duration and edit freedom

Target duration for the independent story promo: **approximately 20–30 seconds** for the first production candidate.

This is not a rigid shot count.

The edit may breathe more than the factual report. Individual shots can hold longer when performance or atmosphere carries the moment.

The promo does not need to show every family member equally or touch every plot beat. It needs to establish one coherent emotional promise of the story.

## 10. Soundtrack / Atlas audio identity

A dedicated **Atlas soundtrack / sonic brand language is intentionally deferred** as a separate brand-making task.

For the first story-promo candidate:
- use temporary cinematic sound/music only if needed to evaluate pacing and emotion;
- do not treat placeholder music as final Atlas branding;
- preserve enough edit flexibility to replace the temporary bed later without rebuilding the visual story.

## 11. Relationship to the hybrid opening — LOCKED

The story promo is produced and approved **independently first**.

Only after both source assets are approved does production create:

`AOC001-REPORT-VIDEO + AOC001-STORY-PROMO → AOC001-OPEN-01`

The hybrid opening will select the strongest factual moments from the report and strongest human moments from this promo.

Therefore the story promo should contain at least one powerful final human image capable of becoming the hybrid opening's hand-off frame, but this requirement must not distort the story.

## 12. Story-promo failure conditions

A candidate fails if:
- it introduces new plot for excitement;
- it changes the approved family or character relationships;
- character identity drifts between shots;
- Calama looks generic or culturally incoherent;
- it becomes an AI demo reel instead of a story teaser;
- it relies on spectacle rather than the family's emotional tension;
- it explains too much through text;
- it looks like factual reportage without a clear fiction label;
- generated imagery contradicts the approved story/authenticity locks;
- visual polish is achieved at the cost of story truth.

## 13. Production sequence — ONE STEP AT A TIME

1. **LOCK THIS BRIEF.**
2. Build the **character visual reference set / character bible** for Daniela, Mauricio, Tomás, Emilia and Elena.
3. Human review of character identity and family coherence.
4. Build a short shot plan using only approved story material.
5. Generate the moving promo candidate.
6. Run continuity, story-truth and visual-quality QA.
7. Human review.
8. Only after approval, use selected story-promo moments in `AOC001-OPEN-01`.

Do not generate the full promo before the character reference set passes human review.

---

**Locked production principle:**

> **The factual report is constrained by evidence. The fictional promo is constrained by story truth. Inside that boundary, let the filmmaking breathe.**
