## Scene entry
**ON SCREEN:** BEFORE THE RIG MOVES

**VOICE / TEXT:** “The location is ready for handover. At least, that is what the schedule says.”

A civil supervisor stands beside a site office. The next crew wants access. The supervisor has construction records, but the story does not tell us whether the records are complete. The question is what the supervisor should verify.

**CHOICE:** What do you do before signing the handover?

### A — Ask for the evidence
“Show me the ground assessment, the construction records and the rig loads this platform was designed to carry.”

**IMMEDIATE CONSEQUENCE:** The handover pauses while the documents are checked. A delay is possible; so is catching a mismatch before the rig arrives.

**NEXT PERSPECTIVE:** Wellsite engineer — who can confirm whether the foundation and rig loads match?

### B — Accept the handover on the information available
“The crew is waiting. I’ll sign with the records we have.”

**IMMEDIATE CONSEQUENCE:** Mobilisation may proceed sooner, but unresolved assumptions travel with the paperwork. This does **not** establish that a failure will occur.

**NEXT PERSPECTIVE:** Drilling crew member — what happens when uncertainty appears during operations?

### C — Escalate the uncertainty
“I can't verify the load basis. The operator and drilling contractor need to agree on the next step.”

**IMMEDIATE CONSEQUENCE:** Responsibility for the decision becomes explicit, though a meeting is not a substitute for technical verification.

**NEXT PERSPECTIVE:** Contractor liaison — how do three organisations make sure they understand the same issue?
