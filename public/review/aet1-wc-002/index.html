When the Heat Comes Inside

When the Heat Comes Inside: The Changing Burden of Heat in Dhaka

FACT
In Dhaka, Bangladesh, rising heat has evolved from an intermittent weather challenge into a persistent constraint shaping daily life, particularly for working households and children. Where extreme heat was once an episodic threat, recent evidence documents its spread into almost every aspect of the city’s routine – from the factory floor to home life and schooling.

FACT
This change is grounded in new, direct studies covering Dhaka’s formal and informal work sectors, worker households, and national labor patterns. Surveys and observational research show heat imposing physical, economic, and recovery burdens across the working population, and reaching children through school closures and household stress.

Multiple hazards contribute to some observed outcomes, and not all mechanisms or future trajectories are resolved; the documented adaptation and burden reduction is limited to pragmatic and partial fixes achieved in specific studies.

The Heat

Heat as a Persistent Condition

FACT
Heat in Bangladesh has shifted from an occasional hazard to a condition that now shapes the reality of work and life day after day. National analysis using data from over 16,000 people finds that prolonged heat is associated with both physical and mental health effects and contributed to an estimated 250 million lost workdays in 2024.

WB-HEAT-2025

Urban Form and the Human Heat Burden

FACT
Dhaka’s dense urban form and high humidity amplify the heat experienced by residents, transforming air temperature into a more intense human burden. This is reflected in measured urban heat index conditions, where the city’s layout and atmospheric moisture mean that the effects of heat are consistently compounded beyond what air temperature readings alone would suggest.

The Shift

Heat in the Working Day: Strain and Capacity

FACT
Worker-reported evidence from garment factories reveals that daily heat leads to symptoms such as headaches, dizziness, fatigue, and nausea, with hydration needs and physical breaks reducing productivity. Surveyed informal-worker households similarly report missed work and health burdens, driven by exposure that continues throughout the working day.

RMG-WORKER-2025 IIED-HEAT-2026

Factories and the Long Heat Season

FACT
Evidence from eight Dhaka-region apparel factories shows that indoor heat is not isolated from outdoor temperatures. A seven-month heat-stress season was observed, with persistently high indoor temperatures meaning that workers are exposed to heat stress well beyond the hottest weeks. These findings apply to the sampled factories and do not represent the entirety of factory experience in Bangladesh.

CORNELL-HEAT-2026

Heat, Income, and Informal Outdoor Work

FACT
For informal outdoor workers surveyed in Dhaka, avoiding the midday heat often means forgoing income, as missed hours are rarely compensated and the necessity to rest or adapt one’s schedule results in tangible economic loss. These findings reflect the experiences within the surveyed population and timeframe.

The Pay Packet

From Lost Workdays to Household Pressure

FACT
Surveyed households among Dhaka’s informal workers documented a pattern where lost workdays due to heat exposure translated directly into pressure on household income. The extent of income loss varies and should not be extrapolated to all households in the city.

IIED-HEAT-2026

Heat’s National Economic Burden

FACT
On a national scale, Bangladesh lost an estimated 250 million workdays in 2024 due to heat exposure, according to modelled analysis. This magnitude of lost labor has implications for the broader economy, though such estimates are derived from modeling rather than direct observation.

The Home

Heat at Home: Beyond the Factory Floor

FACT
Research in worker homes and household surveys in Dhaka reveals that for many, leaving the workplace does not end exposure to heat. Homes in surveyed areas often lack effective insulation or cooling, extending the period of discomfort and recovery and blending work-derived heat stress with nightly rest.

Unequal Recovery and Household Resources

UNCERTAIN
While there are documented differences in the capacity of households to cope with heat based on housing quality, access to cooling, and household resources, the evidence does not provide a full accounting of every strategy or expenditure. The exact nature of household adaptation remains partially unresolved within the available evidence.

The Next Generation

Heat Disrupts Schooling and Child Health

FACT
Heatwaves in Bangladesh during 2024 led to nationwide school closures and disrupted learning for millions of children. The effects of heat exposure during these periods extended to student health and contributed to a broader pattern of climate-related education disruption.

UNICEF-EDU-2025

The full impact at the family level in Dhaka remains an open question.

The Fix

Physical and Operational Adaptation

FACT
Tested adaptations in factories and worker households include practical interventions such as shading, improved airflow, hydration, and changes in work practices. The evidence directly documents these responses within the scope of the studied sites and households.

CORNELL-HEAT-2026 IIED-HEAT-2026

Cooling Investments with Measured Returns

FACT
In a sample of Dhaka-region apparel factories, some investments in cooling were found to repay themselves within approximately one to four years, measured against reduced heat-related productivity losses. These returns are specific to the study settings and are not general guarantees.

CORNELL-HEAT-2026

Who Gets Protection? Questions of Distribution

UNCERTAIN
The evidence supports that access to adaptation measures—whether in the workplace or at home—is unevenly distributed. Not every worker or household can afford or secure protection from heat. The future distribution of adaptation costs and residual exposures remains unresolved in this evidence bundle.

STORY / FICTION

The Room After Work

Maya, Rafiq, Nila, their flat, dialogue, schedule decisions and events are invented. The surrounding heat, worker strain, income risk, household exposure and school disruption are evidence-established conditions; comparative/ordinary-human knowledge supports the fictional connections.

By the time Maya reaches the stairwell, the heat of the factory has changed shape but not disappeared. Outside it was traffic, concrete and wet air. Inside the flat it has been waiting in the walls.

Nila is on the floor beside the doorway with a workbook open. School has been disrupted by the heat. In this story, that means she is home before Maya expected her. The closure is grounded in documented heat-related school disruption; this family's afternoon is invented.

Rafiq has moved the small fan so it points toward Nila. Maya stands in front of it anyway for ten seconds. Nila protests. Maya laughs, then gives it back.

There is rice to wash. Maya sits first. Her shirt is still damp at the collar. At work she drank water whenever she could. She has the dull heaviness that comes after a day spent negotiating with heat.

Rafiq asks whether she will take tomorrow off if the night stays this hot. Maya asks whether tomorrow's pay will take itself off too.

It is not a speech. It is arithmetic. A missed day can mean less money. Going in can mean carrying fatigue back through the same door tomorrow night.

Nila asks whether school will open. Neither adult knows. Rafiq says they will work it out. Maya looks at the fan, then at the window, then at her daughter.

Later, after the cooking, the three of them arrange themselves around the moving air. Nila falls asleep first. Maya and Rafiq talk quietly about whether one of them can change hours if school closes again. They disagree about what is practical, then stop because they are too tired to keep disagreeing.

Nothing catastrophic happens. That is the point. The heat does not need a dramatic ending to reorganise a day. It can take a little work, a little money, a little patience and a little sleep, then come back tomorrow.

FICTION BOUNDARY

These people, dialogue and events are invented. This is not testimony.

Consequences: What Is Known and What Remains Uncertain

FACT
