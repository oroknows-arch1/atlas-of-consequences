01 · WORKLOAD · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

AI begins as software. Running it requires machines.

Training and using AI models happens mainly in data centres. The workload ultimately has to be processed by physical computing hardware.

Evidence · IEA · Energy and AI
02 · ACCELERATION · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

AI changes what sits inside the rack.

Servers can use conventional CPUs and specialised accelerators such as GPUs. The IEA says rising AI adoption is accelerating deployment of high-performance accelerated servers.

Evidence · IEA · accelerated servers
03 · SYSTEM

The machine is larger than the chip.

Data centres combine servers with storage, networking, power systems and cooling. Servers account for around 60% of electricity demand in modern data centres on average, although the share varies substantially by facility type.

Evidence · IEA · data-centre components
04 · DENSITY · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

More capability can concentrate more power into the same physical space.

The IEA reports that AI-server power density rose sharply from 2020 to 2025 and expects further increases. Hardware and software efficiency are also improving, so future energy demand depends on both efficiency and how quickly AI use and capabilities expand.

Evidence · IEA 2026 · direction and uncertainty
05 · PHYSICAL HAND-OFF · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

At the edge of the technology system, computation becomes an infrastructure question.

Servers cannot operate without electricity, cooling and network connections. As AI-focused computing expands, its physical requirements connect the Technology perspective directly to Energy + Grid.

Connection · Technology → Energy + Grid
TECHNOLOGY · PERSPECTIVE COMPLETE

This route ends here.

AI workloads depend on physical computing systems, and AI adoption is changing the mix and density of hardware inside data centres. That does not mean every AI task, model or facility has the same hardware or energy footprint; efficiency, utilisation and deployment patterns vary and continue to change.

← PerspectivesENERGY + GRID
01 · LOAD

AI begins as computation. The grid experiences it as demand.

Data centres contain servers, storage, networking and cooling systems. The IEA estimates they consumed about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024—around 1.5% of global electricity consumption.

Evidence · IEA · Energy and AI
02 · GROWTH

The load does not stay inside the building.

The IEA expects global data-centre electricity use to more than double by 2030 in its base case, with AI an important driver alongside other digital services. Where new capacity connects matters because electricity systems are physical and local.

Evidence · IEA · projection, not certainty
03 · CONNECTION

Generation has to supply it. Grids have to connect it.

Growing data-centre capacity can require transmission and distribution connections, substations, transformers, cables and other equipment. Rapid expansion can therefore create pressure beyond the facility itself.

Evidence · IEA · grid infrastructure
04 · BOTTLENECK

The constraint can move upstream.

The IEA identifies grid-equipment supply constraints as an increasingly urgent issue alongside rapid AI and data-centre expansion. More electricity demand is not automatically the same thing as more available grid capacity.

Evidence · IEA · supply constraints
05 · MATERIAL SYSTEM

The grid turns energy demand into material demand.

Electricity infrastructure uses physical equipment and materials, including copper. This is the hand-off to the Minerals + Resources perspective: the same change can now be followed through a different lane.

Connection · Energy + Grid → Minerals + Resources
ENERGY + GRID · PERSPECTIVE COMPLETE

This route ends here.

AI and data-centre growth can add electricity demand and increase pressure on grid connections and equipment. It does not prove that every data centre creates the same load, that every grid faces the same constraint, or that AI alone caused a particular local infrastructure project.

← PerspectivesMINERALS + RESOURCES
01 · COMPUTE

The question feels weightless. The system answering it is not.

AI models run inside physical infrastructure: servers, storage, networking and cooling. The IEA estimates data centres used about 415 TWh of electricity in 2024, around 1.5% of global electricity consumption.

Evidence · IEA · SR-01–03
02 · GRID + MATERIALS

More computation reaches beyond the server rack.

Growing capacity needs electricity connections, transformers, substations, cables and other grid equipment. Copper is one of the materials used across data centres and the wider electricity system.

Evidence · IEA · SR-02–04
03 · GEOGRAPHY

A global demand system becomes local somewhere.

Copper is not an “AI metal.” AI and data-centre growth enters a much larger demand system spanning grids, buildings, transport, industry and electrification.

Boundary · connection, not singular causation
04 · CHILE

The chain narrows toward a major copper-producing country.

Chile is one of the world’s major copper producers. Within Chile, Antofagasta is the dominant production centre; Cochilco projected it at 58% of national copper production in 2025.

Evidence · Cochilco · SR-05–06
05 · ANTOFAGASTA

Desert, coast, cities and mines share one regional system.

This establishes a place where global material demand meets an existing copper economy. It does not establish that AI caused a particular mine expansion.

Evidence boundary preserved
06 · CALAMA / CHUQUICAMATA

The material chain arrives beside a real city.

Chuquicamata lies about 15 km north of Calama. Codelco reports 266 thousand tonnes of fine copper produced in 2025 and 3,992 direct employees at year end. Calama itself is more than a mine: it is a city of households, schools, businesses and ordinary life.

Evidence · Codelco + UCN · SR-07–12
MINERALS + RESOURCES · PERSPECTIVE COMPLETE

This route ends here.

AI/data-centre growth adds pressure inside a much larger electricity and copper demand system. This route does not claim AI caused an existing Antofagasta mine expansion, a particular job, or the fictional events in the Daniela and Mauricio story.

01 · PLACE · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

A global material system arrives beside ordinary households.

Calama is a city of more than 166,000 people in the Antofagasta Region, with Chuquicamata roughly 15 km away. Mining is part of the surrounding economic and social landscape, but the city is not reducible to the mine.

Evidence · INE + Codelco · local context
02 · ROSTER · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

Work can reorganise time at home.

Research on mining commuting in northern Chile documents roster systems including 7×7 schedules and their effects on family and social life. A work opportunity can therefore carry household trade-offs as well as income.

Evidence · UCN · mining commuting and family life
03 · STORY · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

Daniela and Mauricio let the edition explore that tension at human scale.

Their household is fictional. It can explore absence, opportunity, parenting and pressure informed by documented local conditions, but nothing that happens to this family is evidence that it happened to a real Calama household.

STORY / FICTION · evidence-informed, not evidence
04 · CONSEQUENCE · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

The consequence is not automatically good or bad.

Mining-linked work can support households while rostered absence can reshape routines and relationships. Atlasoquence keeps both possibilities visible rather than converting a structural trend into a predetermined family outcome.

Interpretation · bounded by local evidence

01 · EXISTING WORK

The labour system exists before the AI story reaches it.

Chuquicamata reported 3,992 direct employees at the end of 2025 and 266 thousand tonnes of fine copper production that year. These are existing mining facts, not jobs created by AI.

Evidence · Codelco · 2025
02 · ROSTERS · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

Mining work is organised through time as well as skill.

Research on mining commuting in northern Chile documents roster arrangements including 7×7 schedules. Work therefore connects labour markets to transport, absence, recovery time and household routines.

Evidence · UCN · mining commuting
03 · CAPABILITY · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

More demand does not automatically mean a particular job.

Any future pressure from data centres, grids or copper demand must pass through real projects, employers, investment decisions and required capabilities before it becomes employment. The route does not infer a skills shortage or a promotion from commodity demand alone.

Boundary · demand → project → work is not automatic
04 · OPPORTUNITY · VISUAL ASSET REQUIRED

Opportunity is a possibility inside the system, not a guaranteed consequence.

Mauricio's work opportunity belongs to STORY. It can explore what changing demand might mean for a worker and family while remaining separate from documented employment at Chuquicamata.

STORY / FICTION · no causal claim

01 · CAPITAL

The AI build-out is already a capital story.

Global investment in data centres reached about US$500 billion in 2024, according to the IEA. The physical expansion behind AI therefore enters markets through capital spending as well as electricity demand.

Evidence · IEA · 2025
02 · MATERIAL EXPOSURE

Capital spending propagates into equipment and materials.

Data-centre construction and grid expansion require physical equipment and minerals including copper. AI is one additional source of pressure inside a much larger electrification and infrastructure demand system.

Evidence · IEA · critical minerals and grids
03 · CONCENTRATION

Demand becomes financially relevant where supply and exposure concentrate.

Chile is a major copper producer and Antofagasta is the country's dominant copper-producing region. Concentration can make geography important to commercial interpretation, but it does not by itself establish a company valuation or investment case.

Evidence · Cochilco · structural exposure
04 · INTERPRETATION · FINANCIAL EVIDENCE REQUIRED

A structural story is not yet an investment decision.

Company exposure, financial quality, valuation, entry price and risk require their own current evidence. Atlasoquence will not turn the physical demand chain into a buy, hold or sell conclusion without that layer.

Evidence state · financial layer not yet attached
MARKETS + FINANCE · PERSPECTIVE COMPLETE

This route ends here.

The evidence establishes a capital-intensive AI build-out and a path into electricity infrastructure and materials. It does not establish that copper is an “AI metal,” that a particular producer benefits, or that any security is attractively valued.
