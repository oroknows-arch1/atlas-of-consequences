AI has a physical supply chain.

Start with the infrastructure needed to run computation, then follow one material through the system: copper.

01 · COMPUTE BECOMES INFRASTRUCTURE

The digital system has a physical footprint.

Data centres depend on servers, storage, networking, cooling, backup systems and connections to electricity grids. As data-centre electricity demand grows, expansion reaches beyond software into physical equipment.

Evidence: SR-01, SR-02, SR-03 · IEA

02 · INFRASTRUCTURE NEEDS MATERIAL

Grid expansion pulls metals into the story.

The IEA identifies pressure on power-equipment supply chains associated with data-centre expansion and names copper among the materials required for construction and electrical infrastructure.

Evidence: SR-04 · IEA

03 · FOLLOW COPPER

The path reaches Chile.

Copper demand does not begin with AI, and AI is not its only driver. Chile already operates one of the world's major copper systems. Atlas follows the material because additional infrastructure demand enters that existing system rather than creating it.

Evidence: SR-04, SR-06 · IEA + Cochilco

04 · CONCENTRATION

Then the map narrows to Antofagasta.

Cochilco projects the Antofagasta Region at 58% of Chilean copper production in 2025 and above half through 2034. That concentration makes the region materially relevant when following copper through Chile.

Evidence: SR-05 · Cochilco · projection, not observed future production

05 · THE MATERIAL BECOMES A PLACE

Chuquicamata sits beside Calama.

Codelco reports that Chuquicamata, about 15 km north of Calama, produced 266 thousand metric tonnes of fine copper in 2025 and had 3,992 direct employees at year end. The global material chain has now arrived at a specific operating landscape.

Evidence: SR-07, SR-08 · Codelco operational data

What Atlas is not claimingNone of this evidence establishes that AI caused Chuquicamata, a particular Chilean mining investment, or a local employment outcome. AI/data-centre growth is an additional infrastructure pressure entering a much older copper economy.
