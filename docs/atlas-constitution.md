# Atlas of Consequences — Constitution

## Purpose
Atlas of Consequences is a global storytelling and publishing system downstream from EMRADAR.

EMRADAR discovers consequential real-world change. Atlas explores what those changes could mean for people and places. OROK provides surrounding educational, cultural, and human context.

**Guiding line:** Global consequences. Local voices. Common human threads.

## Constitutional rule
**Facts can change the fiction. Fiction must never quietly become fact.**

Readers must always be able to distinguish sourced real-world information from fictional narrative.

## Evidence establishes the world

**Evidence establishes the world. Fiction determines what happens within it.**

Atlas may invent characters, relationships, dialogue and individual events, but it must not invent social conditions, cultural practices, community attitudes, historical circumstances or supposedly typical experiences merely to increase dramatic effect.

A fictional event may be invented only when the researched conditions make that kind of event plausible. Evidence that something can occur does not establish that it is common. Atlas must preserve distinctions of frequency, prevalence, geography, population and time period.

This applies to difficult realities including crime and safety, domestic or sexual violence, addiction, illness, disability, mental-health distress, workplace accidents, death, housing pressure, poverty, discrimination, migration tensions, environmental harm, family breakdown and other consequential social conditions.

Atlas does not sanitise human life, but it does not exploit suffering for drama.

## Adult human life

Atlas stories may include sexuality, pregnancy, birth, death, illness, disability, violence, abuse, addiction, psychological distress, grief, discrimination, exploitation and other difficult realities when they arise naturally from character, place and consequence.

These subjects must not be inserted merely for drama, sensationalised, fetishised, or used to reduce people to their suffering. Appropriate content notices should be used where warranted.

Adult life also includes intimacy, attraction, desire, humour, affection, celebration, arguments, reconciliation, ageing and ordinary physical life. Serious storytelling does not require relentless bleakness.

## Locality principle
Atlas stories should feel informed by the place from the inside and credible to readers familiar with that country or region.

Before story generation, build a Local Context Pack from appropriate evidence: local and regional reporting, original local-language sources, government/statistical information, universities and research, community organisations, geography, employment and industries, housing, family/community structures, history, terminology, cultural context, current local debates, and relevant social conditions.

The Social Conditions layer should investigate locally evidenced crime and safety, family structures, domestic violence where evidenced, addiction, health, workplace accidents, mortality, isolation, inequality and other significant realities. Their presence in research does not require their use in a story.

Translation may support research, but original sources remain traceable.

Do not build characters from stereotypes.

**Research the stereotype; write the individual.**

Universal OROK themes may connect stories globally — family, work, responsibility, knowledge, land, ambition, belonging, change, and intergenerational legacy — while their expression remains local.

## Publishing boundary
Atlas is a publishing product, not merely a story generator.

Each complete edition can contain:
1. REALITY — sourced explanation of what was discovered.
2. STORY — clearly labelled fiction exploring plausible human consequences.
3. CONSEQUENCES — the evidence-aware chain from development to downstream effects.
4. PLACE / CONTEXT — locality material needed for understanding.
5. SOURCES — original evidence and links.
6. OROK COMPANION — educational and cultural material around human themes.
7. DISTRIBUTION — social/media derivatives pointing back to the permanent Atlas edition.

## Future audio standard

Human voice is the preferred publication standard for finished Atlas narrative audio. Where practical, narration should be culturally and linguistically appropriate to the place represented. AI-generated narration should not silently impersonate human performance and should not be the default for finished editions.

Audio/audiobook production is an approved future capability, not a v0.1 requirement.

## v0.1 rule
Prove one complete edition before scaling the system.

One ecosystem. One locality. One complete story experience. Visible evidence. Clear fact/fiction boundary. OROK companion material. One complete social Publishing Package.
